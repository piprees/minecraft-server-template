# Worldgen

One owner: us. Vanilla's noise generator is fast, maintained by Mojang, and on 26.3 almost entirely data; we supply the data. Each dimension picks and tunes pieces from our library and the compiler writes the result ([architecture.md](architecture.md#the-compiler)).

## Layers

| Layer | What a definition chooses | 26.x mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Terrain | Continents, erosion, ridges and peaks, plateaus and terraces, canyons, rivers, islands and archipelagos, floating islands, sky islands joined into rimmed lakes, voids with rims, shattered land, spires, floors under a roof (plains, pits, chasms, corridors, ledges, pillars), and scale and height multipliers | Our named density functions; 26.3's `distance_to_point`, `gradient`, `lerp` and `interval_select` cover islands, rims and bands in data |
| Caves | Cheese, spaghetti and noodle mixes; cave networks; a solid or hollow underground | Density functions and carvers |
| Biomes | A list of vanilla and [library](#biome-library) biomes with climate boxes; stamps by shape; swaps inside a zone or everywhere | Multi-noise layouts the compiler builds without overlaps (26.4's rule); our biome source `customdimensions:zoned` for zones and swaps |
| Materials | `materials`: `top`, `filler`, `deep`, `cliffs`, `beaches`, `ceilings`, `defaultBlock`, `biomes` (a palette per biome id or `#tag`) and `bands` (a palette per depth: each `below` a y, deepest first) | The dimension's own `worldgen/material_rule`: the preset's bedrock and veins, each band under `not y_above`, each biome's palette under a `minecraft:biome` condition (the definition's, then each listed library biome's own, then a listed vanilla Nether biome's own ground off the nether preset), the dimension's palette, on the caves preset a sunless floor, then the preset's rule |
| Liquids and aquifers | `liquids`: `seaLevel`, `lavaLevel`, `defaultFluid`, `aquifers` (`on`, `off`, `flooded`, `dry`) | Noise settings' `sea_level`, `default_fluid` and top-level `aquifers` object; `lavaLevel` has no data field (the generator's -54 is code), so the runtime replaces that level's fluid rule |
| Ores | `ores`: `veins` (`true`, `false` or a list `like` copper or iron), `add` (ores as placed features), `remove` (placed feature ids) | Veins are `minecraft:ore_vein` material rules; added and removed placed features reach one dimension through the runtime's per-level biome feature lists, so no biome is copied; a library biome keeps its own list |
| Zones | Terrain, biomes, aquifers and structure wants replaced inside a shape | A mask density function per zone; terrain and aquifers lerp in the router; biomes, materials and ores follow it through our biome source |
| Atmosphere | Fog, sky, clouds, light, particles, music, `atmosphere.dayLength` (`dayMinutes`, `nightMinutes`; `settings.json`'s `defaults.atmosphere.dayLength` is 45 and 15), beds and anchors | Environment attributes and timelines. A day length rewrites each timeline that repeats in whole vanilla days (`day`, `moon`, `villager_schedule`) under `<namespace>:day_length/<name>_<dayTicks>_<nightTicks>`: vanilla ticks 0–12000 stretch over the day minutes and 12000–24000 over the night, keyframes, time markers and the period alike, and numeric and colour tracks gain a sample every 500 vanilla ticks so the sun's eased arc follows the warp (`DayLength.java`). The warped timelines read a world clock of their own, `<namespace>:day_length/<dayTicks>_<nightTicks>`, which becomes the dimension type's `default_clock` (a clock's time markers must be unique across every timeline on it, and the warped day carries vanilla's). A clock's time is the server's, not a level's: every dimension on one day length shares its sun, and `time set` in any of them moves them all; a `fixedTime` dimension keeps its frozen timelines on `minecraft:overworld`. Random ticks, weather and crop growth keep vanilla's pacing: they read no timeline |
| Structures | `structures`: density, noise groups, wants and shuns, forced starts, replacements, spacing, mode, terrain adaptation | The compiler's `structure_control/<slug>.json`, applied to the level's structure state over every set the firewall keeps ([below](#structure-control)) |

Generator variants (M3) are starting points any definition tunes: normal, large biomes, amplified, caves, floating islands, Nether and End, plus flat with layers, checkerboard, void and single-biome. Heights are linted to Bedrock's limits. A void listing more than one biome (or a `biomeLayout`) is a noise generator on the overworld's climate whose final density is -1: its biomes are laid out as a noise world's, by climate box or regions, with no block, no aquifers and its sea at the bottom of the world; its biomes' features still run, so one that needs no ground can still place. A checkerboard's `generator.voidBiomes` holds the router's final density at -1 in those biomes' cells (`customdimensions:checker_cells` computes vanilla's cell index, `floorMod((x >> (scale + 4)) + (z >> (scale + 4)), count)`), so they carry no terrain; a zone over such a cell changes its biome, not its void.

A preset's material rule lays ground only for the biomes it was written for. A vanilla Nether biome listed on any preset but `nether` takes its own ground as a palette after the definition's and the library's (a definition's `materials.biomes` entry for it wins): `crimson_forest` crimson nylium and `warped_forest` warped nylium over netherrack, so their fungi stand; `soul_sand_valley` soul sand over soul soil; `nether_wastes` netherrack; `basalt_deltas` basalt over blackstone with blackstone cliffs. The caves preset runs vanilla's overworld surface with no `above_preliminary_surface` gate, so every biome without a floor of its own falls to `biome_surface/default` and grows grass on every dry cave floor; there, after the dimension's palette, `minecraft:moss_block` tops the dry floors of every biome outside the ones vanilla's `overworld/biome_surface` names, or an `overworld/surface` clause lays a block other than water for (desert, badlands, mushroom fields, dripstone caves and the like keep theirs). A dimension `materials.top` replaces it, so `grass_block` there asks for grass. `materials.bands` lays palettes by depth: each band holds below its `below` y and wins over the per-biome palettes and the dimension's, the deepest listed first.

### Terrain library

A definition picks one terrain in `terrain.base` and tunes it: `setting` (`gentle`, near vanilla; `default`; `extreme`) gives starting values, `params` overrides any of them, and `scale` (0.25–4, horizontal stretch of every terrain noise and of the biome climate) and `heightMultiplier` (0.25–3, multiplies heights about sea level) apply to all. `scales` (each 0.25–4) multiplies `scale` per router noise: `continents`, `erosion`, `ridges` and `climate` (temperature and vegetation). `layout` adds a large shape to continentalness (`radial`, `ring` or `linear`, with `strength` 0–1). `climateShift` (`temperature`, `vegetation`, each −1–1, 0) adds to the router's climate, and `climateWarp` (`blocks` 0–1024, 0; `wavelength` 64–4096, 512) moves the point it is sampled at, both described [below](#climate-shift-and-warp). Without a `base`, `scale`, `scales` and `layout` shape the `overworld`, `large_biomes` and `amplified` presets' own terrain; the `nether`, `caves` and `end` presets' own terrain takes `scales.climate`, `climateShift` and `climateWarp` alone, which stretches the temperature and vegetation their biomes are laid out on (the Nether's own, or the overworld's a caves or End biome list borrows) and nothing else. How to choose them: [placeness.md](placeness.md). An unknown terrain or parameter, or a value outside its range, stops the compile with the definition's slug in the message; `schema/definition.schema.json` carries each terrain's parameters with `minimum`, `maximum` and `default`.

Each terrain is built from vanilla's overworld router (`compiler/.../terrain/`). It shapes a surface height in blocks and how closely the ground follows it, and replaces vanilla's `offset` (height / 128 − 1), `factor`, `jaggedness` and `sloped_cheese`; a terrain may add a 3D term that removes ground (voids, cracks) or adds it (sky islands). Caves, aquifers, ore veins and the slides stay vanilla's, so every surface lies between y −64 and 256, inside Bedrock's overworld range. The router's continentalness, erosion, ridges, temperature and vegetation are the preset's own, stretched by `scale`, and the shoreline sits where vanilla's biomes expect the coast, so biomes follow the land. The compiler writes `<namespace>:terrain/<slug>/` with `height`, `offset`, `factor`, `jaggedness`, `depth`, `sloped_cheese`, `final_density`, `preliminary_surface_level` and `chunk_surface_level`, the ids a zone blends between, and the library's noises as `<namespace>:terrain/<noise>`.

`overworld`, `large_biomes`, `amplified` and `floating_islands` take a terrain; `checkerboard` takes one on overworld noise, and `single_biome` on its `preset` (overworld unless it names one). `caves` and `nether` refuse every terrain but `roofed` (their terrain is a cavern under a ceiling, not a surface), and `end` refuses every terrain but `end` (`minecraft:the_end` reads the router's erosion as its island shape, so only a terrain that shapes the island density itself fits there). An airborne terrain (`floating_islands`, `basin_islands`, `voids`) has no aquifers, no solid floor, and its sea level at the bottom of the world unless `liquids.seaLevel` sets one; a sea level over a void fills the void below it, so `basin_islands` holds its lakes with `liquids.basins` instead, which fills only its basins.

A canyon floor lies `depth` below the land at its upper reaches. A low-frequency grade noise, and the coast, lower it toward sea level by `grade` of the way; the floor is stepped `cascadeStep` apart into flat reaches with a dry fall between each, so floors reach sea level only at their low ends and where canyons meet the coast. Where the grade noise lies in its lowest `pools` share the floor sinks 4 blocks into a hollow, which holds water where the floor is at sea level. Inside the channel vanilla's 3D noise is weighed down to a sixteenth and jaggedness falls away, so the ground follows the height: floors stay flat and walls steep. The factor stays the land's, so caves start as deep below a canyon floor as below the land ([T149](../../../TROUBLESHOOTING.md#t149)).

`roofed` keeps the preset's router and replaces one reference in its final density: vanilla's slides (the bedrock floor and roof) and squeeze stay round `<namespace>:terrain/<slug>/cavern` where they wrapped `minecraft:nether/base_3d_noise`, so the preset's biomes, sea, fluid and climate are its own. `cavern` is solid below `floor` and above `ceiling`, an eighth of density per block, plus vanilla's Nether 3D noise times 3 × `roughness`; `pillars` are solid from the world's floor to its roof. The floor is `floorHeight` plus the relief, then pits, corridors and chasms lower it in that order and `ledges` steps the result. `terrain.layout` places the pits: its shape times 3 × `strength` standard deviations moves the pit noise, so `radial` with a small `pit` is one pit round `centre` (a layout without `pit` is an error); `scale` stretches the terrain's noises and the climate, `scales.climate` the climate alone, and `heightMultiplier` and the other `scales` are refused. The router's `depth` is (`floorHeight` − y) / 128, so a listed biome with `climate.depth` `[0.2, 1]` takes the floors 26 or more blocks under `floorHeight` (pits, corridors, chasms) and the rest of the list the floor above. Under the lava or water sea at `sea_level` (`liquids.seaLevel`) lies whatever sinks below it: the relief's hollows below `floorHeight` − `floorRelief`, pit floors at `floorHeight` − `pitDepth`, corridor beds at `floorHeight` − `corridorDepth`, chasm floors from `floorHeight` − `floorRelief` − `chasmDepth`; keep those above the sea for a dry floor, and the compile report says which lie under it. A zone takes `roofed` on these presets and blends its final density and depth like any terrain.

`end` keeps the end preset's router too and replaces `minecraft:end/sloped_cheese` in its final density with `<namespace>:terrain/<slug>/sloped_cheese`, which is `<prefix>/islands` plus vanilla's `end/base_3d_noise` plus the ridges. `<prefix>/islands` is vanilla's `end/islands` (only `end_outer_islands` with `generator.endIsland` false) plus `coverage`. Under a layout, where its shape is positive the density blends (shape × `strength`) into islands of the terrain's own, the `islands` noise at 1.6 times its frequency (islands about 80 blocks across) scaled 0.5 per standard deviation, less 0.12, plus `coverage`, so islands rise even inside vanilla's empty ring; where the shape is negative, 0.4 × `strength` is taken off (`EndTerrain.laidOut`): `radial` gathers a cluster round `centre` and clears the sky past the falloff, `ring` sets islands round a hollow. Without a layout the islands are vanilla's. It is also the router's `erosion`, which the End's biome source reads, so highlands lie where the shaped density is above 0.25 and midlands above -0.0625. `peaks` adds a ridge of the `detail` noise times 3 × `peaks` where the density lies above 0, full from 0.25; `islandHeight` moves the top slide (vanilla's y 56 to 312) up by that many blocks. `scales.climate` stretches the borrowed overworld climate of a biome list; the other `scales` and `heightMultiplier` are refused.

`spires` takes `terrain.floor`. `land` is the shared land with columns inland. `sea` lays the sea floor at `seaFloor` and raises every column to `landHeight` + `spireHeight` × its profile out of the sea, aquifers kept; the router's continentalness is 0.2 on a column and -0.6 off one, so land biomes take the columns and ocean biomes the sea. `void` and `islands` are terrains over a void (open floor, no aquifers, sea level at the bottom): a `void` column stands from the bottom of the world with nothing between columns; an `islands` column stands on a domed island of its own at `landHeight`, 0.8 deviations of the spire noise wider than the column, 6 blocks of dome and 18 of underside plus a quarter of the spire's height. Off the land `terrain.layout` moves the spire noise as it moves the islands of `floating_islands`.

`floating_islands` and `basin_islands` share five tier parameters: `tiers` 1–4, 1 (island layers counting the terrain's own); `tierSpacing` 32–200, 64 (blocks from one tier's altitude to the next); `tierGap` 16–64, 20 (open sky always left over the tier below); `tierShrink` 0.25–1, 0.6 (each tier's size, thickness and underside against the one below); `tierCoverage` 0–0.8, 0 (each further tier's coverage; 0 is the coverage below times `tierShrink`). A further tier is plain floating islands with the same altitude spread, its islands noise at 1.31 times the frequency of the one below besides its size, so its islands hang over lower islands and over open void alike. Its underside is cut back to `tierGap` over the top below it (`<prefix>/tier<k>_bottom`), and an island with nothing left is gone; on `basin_islands` the top below is the mass's rim and basin floor as well as its islands. `tierGap`'s floor, 16, is the survey's gap between a column's blocks (`PatchAnalysis.GAP`), so every tier reads as a sky layer. The compiler refuses a stack whose top tier's altitude plus its thickness (and terrace lift) passes the dimension's top, and a `tierSpacing` under `tierGap` + 8. Above the midpoint of a gap the place cue and `island_top` are the upper tier's own, below it the lower tier's, so biome regions follow each tier's islands and an `underside` biome hangs under each. `crust`, carvers, liquids and `landShare` are as for one layer: carvers cut every tier, a `liquids.seaLevel` floods the void under the lowest, `liquids.basins` fills only the basins of `basin_islands`' own layer, and `landShare` stays refused over a void.

`flatness` shapes a `floating_islands` top as `thickness` × √min(1, core / (1 − 0.85 × flatness)): the dome at 0, a table reaching its full thickness over the first 0.15 of the islands noise's core at 1. `terraces` 1 floors the layer under the top (altitude plus spread) into treads `terraceStep` apart from `altitude`, rising over the last 0.15 of each step, so a mass tilted by `spread` becomes a stair of plateaus; 2 or more first lifts patches of it, read from the detail noise at the islands' frequency, by up to `terraces` − 1 levels. The survey's landform classifier files a sky island's level ground by its tilt (64-block relief and the 256-block topographic index): at a low `spread` a dome already reads as plains and a flat top keeps most of it so, its roll-off reading as hills round the rim, while at a `spread` of 24 under half the land reads level whatever the top (`LandformsTopsTest`). Treads and terraces read as hills along their risers.

`spires` on `floating_islands` raises columns on the lowest tier's island tops where the spire noise lies in its highest `spires` share, as `spires` raises them on land: `spireHeight` (times `heightMultiplier`) at the crest, `spireTaper` from straight-sided to cones, `spireThickness` the spire noise's girth. A spire stands only on held ground, its foot coming onto the island over the islands noise's core 0.05 to 0.25, so none overhangs the void, and every island parameter (coverage, size, flatness, terraces, tiers) keeps its meaning: a few broad islands carry several spires each, where `spires` with `terrain.floor: islands` gives every spire an islet of its own. A tier above clears a spire by its `tierGap` as it clears an island top. The compiler refuses spires whose crest (altitude, thickness, terrace lift and `spireHeight`) passes the dimension's top. `materials` `cliffs` faces the spires' sides as it faces any steep ground.

`hollowDepth` on `basin_islands` with a `ring` layout adds one lake mass filling the ring's hollow: the lakes noise at a point is at least the hollow's own field, a function of distance from the layout's centre that crosses the mass threshold at the hollow's edge (`radius` − `falloff` / 2, where the ring's shape crosses 0), ends the rim's crest 10 blocks inside, reaches the shore at 24 and the heart at 64. The hollow is then laid as any mass: a rim `rimHeight` over the water line, gaps where `gaps` cuts it, islands at `islandShare`, and a floor dropping from `basinDepth` to `hollowDepth` below the water line from the shore to the heart. `liquids.basins` fills it like any basin. `ringLakes` 0 replaces the lakes noise with the hollow's field, so the ring's islands stand round one lake. A hollow needs a ring layout and at least 80 blocks of radius; anything else is refused.

#### Climate shift and warp

`climateShift` adds `temperature` and `vegetation` to the router's values after any stretch, on a base terrain, a preset's own terrain, and the `nether`, `caves` and `end` presets alike. It is a shift, not a clamp: a multi-noise biome source picks the nearest climate box, so a biome whose box lies past the shifted range takes less of the map or none, and the box at the warm or cold end takes all that lies beyond it (+0.3 on temperature warms a cold seed by about one vanilla band; −0.5 freezes all but the hottest land). Region layouts and the biome lists of terrains over a void rank the level's own climate over the border (`ClimateRegions`), so a shift leaves their shares unchanged; it acts on the vanilla multi-noise sources, such as the overworld preset's own. `climateWarp` moves every shifted noise in the temperature and vegetation by the `warp_x` and `warp_z` noises, two deviations of which are `blocks`, in features `wavelength` blocks across (vanilla's shifted noise samples at block × `xz_scale` + shift, so a move of b blocks is b × `xz_scale`). Every climate boundary then wanders like an ecotone: region cuts, which a broad `scales.climate` makes ruler-straight across several islands, and band edges alike, with no biome's share changed. It does not touch zones, whose own edge wanders by `edgeJitter`.

`biomeLayout.places` replaces the router's first region axis (temperature, or humidity when `axes` lead with it) with a place field: one value per place, `-1 + (2i + 1) / n` for place `i` of `n`, so each place's biomes take one column of that axis. The field is a power diagram over one site per place in border units (block / radius over the smaller of the player and generation borders): a point belongs to the place whose `|p - site|² - weight` is least. Its cells are convex, so each place is one region of the square border; the weights are solved on a 256-cell grid until every share is within 0.4 percentage points of its weight. In vanilla functions (`<namespace>:places/<slug>/`) each place `i` contributes `floor((|p - site|² - weight + lift) × scale) × 16 + i`, a `min` chain takes the least and `least - 16 × floor(least / 16)` reads `i` back: every term is an integer below 2^24, so the game's float samplers compute it exactly, and the diagram is resolved to 1/scale (a power of two, 2^13–2^15) border units squared. That is 11 nodes a place plus 7, and with warp and seed choice about 13 n + 40 nodes evaluated per biome lookup (one arrangement's chain runs); n is at most 16. The sampling point moves by the `warp_x` and `warp_z` noises (`climateWarp`'s), two deviations of which are `warp.blocks`, over `warp.wavelength`, tapered to nothing over the last 8 × blocks inside the border, so the map is the identity on the border's edge. With blocks at most 0.15 of the wavelength no point moves faster than about 0.7 of its neighbours (measured on the stand-in noise of the unit tests), so the warped map stays one-to-one and every place one region; sharp corners where several places meet leave specks of a cell or two, under 0.5% of the border. Automatic sites are random points relaxed towards their cells' centroids (rounder places), the places assigned to them by a hill climb that keeps `neighbours` touching, `apart` apart and as many consecutive places as it can sharing a border; four such layouts are solved per definition, and two `xz_scale` 0 noises, one constant per level seed, choose the layout and one of the square's eight symmetries (`range_choice` at the noise's octiles), so the roller's candidate seeds and the server see the same arrangement and every arrangement holds the same shares. `at` and the named arrangements do not vary with the seed.

On `floating_islands` and `basin_islands` `terrain.layout` acts on coverage: its shape times 3 × `strength` deviations moves the islands noise (and on `basin_islands` the lake masses' noise), so `radial` at strength 1 is one mass or a tight cluster round `centre` with an empty sky past `radius` + `falloff`, and `ring` a ring of islands or masses round a hollow. Every terrain over a void also has a place cue, `<prefix>/place`: -1 over the void, -0.4 over a basin's open water, 0.5 on land (the islands noise's core above 0.1, the mass's rim). It becomes the router's continentalness when the biome layout ranks over land (`biomeLayout.over: land`) or gives a biome the `basin` role, and the islands' top (`<prefix>/island_top`) becomes the router's depth, (top − y) / 128, when a biome has the `underside` role.

Every land terrain starts from the shared land, which each terrain may start at its own defaults:

| Shared parameter | Range, default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `coast` | -0.6–0.4, -0.15 | Continentalness at the shoreline; higher is more ocean |
| `seaFloor` | -40–60, 36 | Height of the open ocean floor |
| `landHeight` | 0–200, 80 | Height of the land far inland; under the sea level the land is sea floor, so a low floor pairs with a low `liquids.seaLevel` |
| `relief` | 0–120, 24 | Blocks of hills where erosion is low |
| `roughness` | 0–1, 0.5 | How far 3D noise breaks the surface; 0 smooth, 1 overhangs |

v1's `adventure:wide` is `continents` with `scale` 2.5, `relief` 16 and `roughness` 0.3 (`terrain_continents_wide`); `adventure:compressed` is `continents` with `scale` 0.6, `relief` 90, `roughness` 0.7 and `heightMultiplier` 1.3 (`terrain_continents_compressed`). The gallery fixtures in `mods/custom-dimensions-26/server/src/selftest/gallery/` show every terrain at its three settings (`roofed` on the nether preset, `end` on the end preset), those two, `terrain_scale_{gentle,default,extreme}`, `terrain_ridges_thin`, the land-share and dry-sea fixtures (`terrain_continents_land_share`, `terrain_overworld_land_share`, `terrain_overworld_dry_sea`), and the sky kit: `terrain_end_cluster`, `terrain_floating_islands_radial`, `terrain_floating_islands_tiers`, `terrain_floating_islands_plateaus`, `terrain_basin_islands_ring`, `terrain_basin_islands_tiers` and `terrain_spires_{void,sea,islands}`; `-PterrainGallery=<dir>` loads them all, and `/customdim render <slug>` draws each.

## Zones

A zone replaces layers inside a shape centred on spawn or a point, with a blend and a jittered edge so borders read as landscape, not compass lines. Zones apply in order, each over the ones before it; outside them the definition's own layers hold. A zone converted from a 1.21.1 biome patch is a circle that sets `biomes` only.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `shape` | `circle` (`radius`), `ring` (`inner`, `outer`), `square` (`radius`, half its side), `box` (`halfSize: [x, z]`), `sector` (`radius` or `outer`, `inner`, `angles: [from, to]` in degrees from east towards south), `polygon` (`points`, `[x, z]` corners relative to the centre), or `all`, the whole dimension, for swaps. Default `circle` |
| `centre` | `[x, z]`, or `spawn`: the definition's `spawn` ([T31](../../../TROUBLESHOOTING.md#t31)), else 0, 0 |
| `blend` | Blocks beyond the edge across which the zone fades out, linearly; 0 is a one-block hard edge. Default 32 |
| `edgeJitter` | Blocks the edge wanders: noise added to the distance from the edge, two standard deviations of which are `edgeJitter`, in features of 128 blocks and finer. Default 0 |
| `terrain` | A library terrain (its name, or an object as the definition's `terrain`) |
| `biomes` | The zone's biome list, laid out as the definition's are |
| `swap` | Biome to biome inside the zone; on shape `all`, everywhere and over every zone |
| `mass` | A floating mass: `bottom` and `top` (y) bound a band that is solid inside the zone's shape, added over the terrain beneath as a maximum, so open air lies between it and the ground below; its side lies where the mask is 0.5, mid-blend, and `taper` (0–1, default 0) moves it inwards towards the top, to where the mask is 0.5 + taper / 2, so a wide blend gives a tapering mass. Its blocks are the zone biome's materials: `top` and `filler` on its cap, `ceilings` under it, `deep` through it |
| `liquids.aquifers` | `on` (the dimension's) or `dry`: no aquifer water or lava and no sea in the open cells of the zone and its blend |
| `structures` | `wants` and `shuns` scoped to the zone, for structure control: a top-level want for a structure the zone wants lands where the zone's weight is at least 0.5, and a structure the zone shuns does not start there |

An error names the slug and the zone (`the_x: zones[2]: outer is required for this shape`).

- **Mask:** `<namespace>:zones/<slug>/<index>/mask` is `clamp(1 − (outside + jitter) / blend, 0, 1)`, where `outside` is the distance beyond the shape's edge. Circles, rings, boxes and `all` are vanilla functions (`gradient`, `square`, `sqrt`, `abs`, `max`); sectors and polygons use `customdimensions:zone_distance`, our one density-function type, which stays on the server. The jitter is the 2D noise `<namespace>:zone_jitter`.
- **Terrain:** the zone's terrain compiles under `<namespace>:terrain/<slug>/zone_<index>/`. Across the blend its `sloped_cheese` is lerped to from the layers beneath, inside a copy of their final density, so the ground moves between the two surface heights before the densities are squeezed; over the mask's last quarter, where the surfaces agree, that is lerped to the zone's own final density, which carries its floor and slides. The router's `depth` and `chunk_surface_level` and the aquifers' `surface_level` lerp the same way, so materials and aquifers read the zone's surface.
- **Aquifers:** `dry` sets the aquifers' `exclusion` to 1 and their `surface_level` 64 blocks above the world wherever the mask is above zero, so no aquifer and no sea fills the zone or its blend. The generator places lava below its lava level (-54, or `lavaLevel`) before it consults any aquifer, and an aquifer cell takes the sea from the cells round it, so the runtime's fluid rule (`server/.../level/DryFluidPicker`) places no sea and no lava wherever a dry zone's compiled mask is above zero and 16 blocks beyond it (one aquifer cell, more than water's spread): the rule reads the compiled mask's still edge and, under the level's seed, the same jitter noise. The aquifer turns any fluid it would still take from a neighbouring cell into air in a covered column, and springs and lakes are not placed there (`NoiseBasedAquiferMixin`, `DryFluidFeatureMixin`). A base sea that meets that line stands at it. A sampler (`/customdim render`, rolls) builds its own copy of a lazy level's generator and applies the same runtime under its seed. Sea level stays one number per dimension.
- **Biomes:** our biome source, `customdimensions:zoned`, wraps the dimension's own. The router's temperature carries the zone whose biomes hold: where zone *c*'s mask reaches 0.5 it is the base temperature plus 16 *c*. The source reads the channel back, takes that zone's layout at the base temperature (or the base's, for a zone that only swaps), applies the zone's swaps, then the global ones. Materials and ores follow the biomes.
- **Biome lists:** a biome with `climate`, or a library biome listed without one (its library `climate`), takes that box first; the others keep vanilla's entries in the preset's parameter list when every one of them has some, and otherwise share temperature by humidity as an even grid. Overlaps are carved away so earlier entries keep their space, which also meets 26.4's rule. The End keeps its own biome source unless it lists biomes; a list there takes the overworld's temperature and vegetation so the layout spreads across the islands.
- **Query:** `core/.../zone/ZoneQuery` answers which zones cover a column, with each mask's still-edge weight. At server start, before any level generates, `server/.../zone/LevelZones` reads every level's zones from the definitions; structure control scopes wants and shuns with their weights, and the fluid rule reads the dry zones.

Normal worldgen everywhere, except a ring of sky islands between 2,000 and 3,000 blocks from spawn (the self-test's `selftest_sky_ring`):

```json
"zones": [
  { "shape": "ring", "centre": "spawn", "inner": 2000, "outer": 3000,
    "blend": 200, "edgeJitter": 64,
    "terrain": { "base": "floating_islands" },
    "biomes": ["minecraft:cherry_grove", "minecraft:meadow"],
    "liquids": { "aquifers": "dry" },
    "structures": { "wants": ["minecraft:ruined_portal"] } }
]
```

## Biome library

Our own biomes, built only from vanilla 26.3 blocks, feature types, particles and sounds: one file per biome, `mods/custom-dimensions-26/compiler/src/main/resources/customdimensions/biomes/<id>.json` ([schema](../../../mods/custom-dimensions-26/schema/biome.schema.json)). Every compile writes each one into the pack as `<namespace>:<id>` (`worldgen/biome`), the namespace being the definitions' `settings.json` `namespace` (`adventure` by default), and a definition lists it in `biomes`, a zone's `biomes` or a `swap` target as it lists a vanilla biome.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `realm` | `overworld`, `caves`, `nether`, `end` or `sky`: where the biome belongs |
| `inspiredBy` | One sentence naming what inspired it, from the list under the [clean-room rule](#clean-room-rule) |
| `differs` | One sentence on how it differs from vanilla in look and behaviour; a biome that is only a remix of vanilla is dropped |
| `biome` | A 26.3 biome file as vanilla writes one (`data/minecraft/worldgen/biome/*.json`): `attributes` (music, ambient sounds, particles, fog and sky colours, mob spawns), `effects`, `temperature`, `downfall`, `has_precipitation`, `carvers` and `features`, at most 11 generation steps of placed feature ids. `"~<name>"` in a step names one of the biome's own features |
| `features` | The biome's own features by name: `feature`, a 26.3 configured feature (`worldgen/feature/*.json` shape) or a vanilla configured feature id, and `placement`, its placement modifiers. They compile to `<namespace>:biome/<id>/<name>` in `worldgen/feature` and `worldgen/placed_feature`; `"~<name>"` inside one names another |
| `palette` | A surface palette, the keys of a definition's `materials` palette. It applies under a `minecraft:biome` condition in every dimension whose biomes, zone biomes or swap targets include the biome, after the definition's own per-biome palettes and before its dimension palette; a definition's own `materials.biomes` entry for the biome wins |
| `climate` | A multi-noise box, as a definition's biome `climate`, used where a definition lists the biome without one; without it the biome shares the even grid |

The compile refuses the library, naming the biome and the field, when a required field is missing, the `realm` is unknown, a `~<name>` names no feature of the biome, or a reference is not vanilla 26.3's: a palette block, a block state's `id`, a feature or placement modifier type, a particle, a sound, a spawned entity, an environment attribute, a carver, a placed feature in `biome.features`, a bare string in a block state provider field (`to_place`, `provider`, `ground_state`, `state_provider`, the tree and huge mushroom providers) that names no vanilla `worldgen/block_state_provider` (26.3 reads such a string as a registered provider, so a block is `{"id": ...}`), or any other `minecraft:` id or tag that no vanilla registry, data file or tag holds. An id in another namespace is refused. What the compiler cannot check is left to the game, which refuses the pack at load: field names and value ranges inside a configured feature or placement, block state property names and values, and attribute value shapes.

At run time a library biome is ours: the firewall compares only `minecraft:` biomes with vanilla's files, so it strips nothing from a library biome, and a definition's `ores.add` and `ores.remove` change the level's vanilla biomes and leave each library biome's feature list as the library wrote it.

## Structure control

The compiler resolves each definition's `structures` block into `data/customdimensions/structure_control/<slug>.json`; every dimension gets one, because noise placement is the default. The runtime applies it to the level's structure state as the chunk map builds it, on top of the firewall's switch-off: base and Fantasy levels alike, never touching the shared registry. Fields: [schema-v2.md § Structures](schema-v2.md#structures).

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Filter | `density: none` and `mode: none` drop every organic set; `mode: allow`/`reject` keep or drop the sets in `list`; `exclude` drops sets |
| Remove and swap | `shuns` (ids, `#tags` or short names) leave every set, so a shunned structure has no candidate chunk anywhere; an `exclusive` forced structure (the default) leaves too; `replace` puts one structure in another's place with its weight |
| Spacing | A set named in `spacing` keeps its own random-spread grid with those values, salt and frequency kept |
| Noise groups | Every other exact random-spread set joins its group (`deco`, `loot`, `settlements`, `maritime`, `dungeons`, `landmarks`, `endgame`; `structure_themes.json`, else inferred from spacing). A group places at the sites of one noise field (the profile's threshold, an exclusion radius scaled by the 10-point `radial` curve, a cleared spawn disc for `dungeons` and `endgame`), its exclusion widened until each structure stays under the repetition ceiling; at each site vanilla picks from the group's structures by weight (times the rarity share) among those whose biome is there. `density` sets every group's profile; `noise` sets one or each. The generator type enables the groups; a peaceful `mobMultiplier` (≤ 0.5) turns `dungeons` and `endgame` off, a hostile one (≥ 2) spreads them evenly. A set whose group the type does not place, concentric rings and other placement types stay as they are |
| Vanilla grids | With `noise: false`, or a type with no groups, `density` scales random-spread sets by theme: dense tightens dungeon and loot spacing to 0.7 and landmark and maritime to 0.85, sparse halves dungeon, loot and landmark frequency and cuts settlement and maritime to 0.7 |
| Forced starts | `force` adds a one-chunk set: the chunk of `x`, `z`, or with `radius` the first of up to 96 chunks within it, in a seeded order, whose biome near sea level suits the structure (else the first). The structure skips the biome check in that chunk only (T53); the structure's own terrain rules still apply, so a structure that needs dry land or a height declines a site without them |
| Wants | Each want resolves to a structure (the first of a tag whose biome the dimension has) and lands as a forced start between `min` and `max` blocks of spawn |
| Included sets | `include` re-admits a set no biome here suits, on its own grid with the biome check skipped |
| Terrain adaptation | `terrainAdaptation` (a structure, a `#tag` or a group) replaces the adjustment the Beardifier uses round that structure in this dimension: `none`, `beard_thin`, `beard_box`, `bury`, `encapsulate`; the 1.21.1 kernels `pedestal` and `platform_skirt` apply as `beard_box`, `ground_blend` as `beard_thin`; `moat` and `drain` have no counterpart and are reported |

Unknown structure and set ids, ids neither vanilla nor a bridge provides, are compile-report warnings; a malformed value stops the compile with the slug. Sizes are measured on 26.3 (`-PmeasureStructures`, [mod README](../../../mods/custom-dimensions-26/README.md#structure-control)): `structure-sizes.json` in the mod's resources for every structure, and each bridge's `size` slots.

## Lessons the kit is built around

- Jigsaw sizes are not footprints: structure sizes are measured, never read from configs; `structure-sizes.json` is re-measured on 26.3 (W4, [T54](../../../TROUBLESHOOTING.md#t54)).
- Dense terrain features vanish into the structure pool, so terrain is shaped by noise, not features ([T55](../../../TROUBLESHOOTING.md#t55)).
- End-style dimensions sample climate at depth ([T76](../../../TROUBLESHOOTING.md#t76)), so a biome list on the end preset is laid out on the overworld's temperature and vegetation (§ Zones, Biome lists).
- First-time generation runs one dimension at a time; the level manager enforces it ([K6](../../../TROUBLESHOOTING.md#k6)).
- All worldgen is creation-time ([D2](../../../TROUBLESHOOTING.md#d2)): a pack rewrite that changes a dimension's noise settings or generator while its region folder holds chunks logs one WARN per dimension (RB-33), and a level with chunks keeps the seed it generated from (RB-37).

## Clean-room rule

Nobody, human or agent, reads Terralith, Incendium, Nullscape, Structory or any other Stardust Labs files while building the kit. Their licence forbids copying parts into another mod and forbids using their work with AI systems. We describe what we want in our own words and build it from vanilla's functions. The inspiration list for our biomes (W7) is Regions Unexplored, Nature's Spirit, Wilder Wild, BetterEnd, BetterNether, Galosphere, YUNG's caves, Underground Worlds and Paradise Lost.

## Firewall and bridges

Any mod may be installed; another worldgen owner may not. At SERVER_STARTING, before any level exists, the mod audits the loaded game and refuses to start, listing each finding as `mod: what where`, if any of these appear without a reviewed bridge:

- a dimension, dimension type or noise setting in a namespace other than `minecraft`, `adventure`, `customdimensions`, `fantasy` and the definitions' own, or a biome source or chunk generator type registered outside them;
- a mixin into worldgen classes from a mod not on the allow list;
- TerraBlender, Biolith or Lithostitched.

Worldgen classes are `net.minecraft.world.level.levelgen..`, `..level.biome..`, `..level.chunk.ChunkGenerator*`, `..level.chunk.status..`, `..level.dimension..` and `net.minecraft.data.worldgen..`, except the runtime classes `PatrolSpawner`, `PhantomSpawner`, `Heightmap`, `Biome`, `MobSpawnSettings` and `EnderDragonFight` (`core/.../firewall/WorldgenClasses`). Mixin targets are read from each mod's `fabric.mod.json`, its mixin configs and each mixin's `@Mixin` annotation.

The allow list (`core/.../firewall/AllowList`):

| Mod | Why |
| --- | --- |
| `c2me*`, `lithium`, `servercore` | Performance; same output |
| `fabric-*` | Fabric API; its hooks act only for mods that call them |
| `customdimensions`, `fantasy` | This mod and its runtime-level library |
| `distanthorizons` | Its LOD hook reads terrain; it does not shape it |
| `moogs_structures` | The library the Moog structure packs need; replaces vanilla structures its config names and adapts terrain around structures |
| `carpet` | Admin tool and the self-tests' fake players; its worldgen hooks sit behind rules that are off by default |

A structure set outside those namespaces and in no reviewed bridge is switched off: it never enters a level's structure state, so it places nothing and `/locate` cannot find it. Placed features and spawns that another mod adds to a vanilla biome, compared with vanilla's own biome file, are removed; each removal is logged once with its biome count. `/customdim audit` lists the allowed mods, unreviewed bridges, switched-off sets and removals.

A bridge ([bridges.md](bridges.md)) is one JSON file per mod in `config/custom-dimensions/bridges/`. It adopts that mod's structure sets and structures (with measured sizes and our tags for mood, climate and depth), features, spawns, reviewed mixin configs and registry entries, and once content starts, its blocks' Polymer and Bedrock mappings. `:core:bridge` writes a first, unreviewed bridge from a structure mod's jar for a human to tag.
