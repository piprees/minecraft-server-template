# Stories

What the world tells, and how. Players arrive long after all of it (RB-44): the world is a setting for their own stories, not a plot. Most of what it says is the **lived-in layer**, small signs of use and time spread lightly everywhere. A few **trails** sit on top: places in several dimensions that share wordless marks, so that a player who happens to notice may think "these are connected". No trail is required, signposted or explained, and nothing in game names a people, place, era or person (RB-43). Canon ids (P, E, L, M, X) are in [canon.md](canon.md); places and their sources in [../places/dimensions.md](../places/dimensions.md).

## The lived-in layer

Small, common, quiet. These are the signs that people lived here, used the land and left; they are what makes a find feel like part of a world rather than a drop. Each is a few blocks, built from the ground it stands on, and most are worn or overgrown.

| Sign | Built from | Where it sits | Worn or grown into |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A worn path | path and coarse dirt in a broken line, gravel on wet ground | between a settlement and its water, over a pass, along a ridge to a view | grass reclaiming it; a stream taking its low end |
| A bench by a view | two stairs with trapdoor arms, no sign | a ridge edge, a lake shore, above a ford | moss on the stone, a missing arm |
| An old fence line | fence posts with gaps, a gate left open | across a slope or a valley floor, following the contour | vines and leaves on the posts, posts alone where the rails rotted |
| A cold hearth | unlit campfire in a ring of stone, a log seat | under an overhang, in a sheltered hollow, beside a path | moss over the ring, a sapling through it |
| A sealed cellar | a trapdoor or door in a hillside, a barrel or chest inside | a south-facing bank near a farm or a ruin | buried door, roots through the ceiling |
| A cairn | a short stack of cobble or the local stone | a summit, a pass, a turn in an old road | tumbled, mossy |
| A well | stone ring, a cauldron or water, a fence frame | the middle of a clearing or a lost village's footprint | dry; filled with gravel; overgrown |
| A lone grave | a mound of coarse dirt, a stone at its head (no words) | a rise above a settlement or a battlefield, under a tree | sunk, a flower on it |
| A woodpile, a charcoal pit | stripped logs; a shallow pit with coal blocks under dirt | the edge of a forest near a camp or a works | logs gone to moss; the pit as a dent |
| A plank bridge, steps cut in a slope | planks over a stream; stairs and slabs up a bank | where a path meets a stream or a rise | a plank missing; steps half buried |
| A fishing place | a stair at the water's edge, a barrel | a calm bank, a jetty stub | the barrel empty, the stair cracked |
| A marker on a road | a lantern on a post over a stone footing | at a fork or a crossing | the lantern gone, the post leaning (fence on a mossy wall) |

Each kind of world has its own version, from its own blocks:

| World | Its quiet signs |
| --- | --- |
| Nether | a chain anchored at a cliff edge with nothing at its end; a wart patch under a basalt overhang; a soul-fire ring gone cold (soul soil, no fire); steps cut into a netherrack bank; a gold nugget left in a pot by a doorway |
| End and void | a purpur step at an island's lip; an end rod marking a crossing; a single chorus stump where a garden was |
| Sky islands | a ladder or chain down an island's edge; a plank landing; a cairn on the highest point |
| Caves and deeps | a ladder up a shaft; a rail end at rock; a minecart under cobwebs; a candle stub on a ledge |
| Water and ice | a mooring post; a hull timber on a beach; a hole cut in the ice with a fence round it |

## Trails

Seven. Each has an anchor (the dimension where the event happened, with most of its places) and satellites (a place or two in other dimensions). A trail's links are its **marks**: the same banner, armour trim, material, arrangement or object in each of its places, and nowhere else. All are vanilla, so every client shows them. Each trail also names the events the world does **not** retell; restraint is part of the design.

### 1. The war nobody won

Canon: E7, E8, M1, M2, P14. Long over; what is left is bones, wreckage and the places people fled to.

- **Anchor: the_boneyard.** The crashed End ships, the giant bones, the craters, the bone pit, the sculk scar (dimensions.md).
- **Satellites:** the_burning_archipelago (wreckage across the straits); the_wailing_narrows (the quarantine wall and its zombie camps); the_bloodroot_wastes (survivors' camps); the_ember_fields (a burned piglin camp as the pacifists' outlier, owner's pick); the_molten_flats (barracks and the arms works); the_soulfields (the graves); the_crumbling_reaches (the End side's crashed ships).
- **Overworld:** nothing of the war itself. One broken-portal study for the_boneyard (bone-block partial frame) deep beside a vanilla fossil.
- **Marks:** on the piglin side, a black banner with the snout pattern in yellow, and remains in gold or netherite armour with the `snout` trim in gold; on the End side, no gear at all, only its materials (purpur rubble, end rods, shulker shells) lying where they fell; `blade` and `danger` sherds in the archaeology.
- **What a player might notice:** the gold-trimmed helmet on remains in the narrows is the one they saw in the boneyard.
- **Not retold:** who started it, or how it ended.

### 2. Below the cities

Canon: E3, E14, P12, P19, X2. The ancient cities, the people who dug into them, and the sculk that woke.

- **Anchor: overworld**, the ancient cities near the border (5632 blocks out and beyond, placement.md).
- **Satellites:** the_underdark (cities, the deepslate camp); the_sculked_beyond (the explorers' last camp); the_souldrift (the breach); the_gauntlet (cities among the peaks).
- **Overworld:** over a few of the rim cities, a dig: a shaft with ladders going down, a camp at its head, and the ground round it part-turned to sculk.
- **Marks:** the explorers' kit, the same in every place: leather armour in one dye with the `wayfinder` trim in copper on remains, a camp chest laid out the same way (a pickaxe, candles, a compass, an unlabelled map with a red X over a city); `explorer` and `howl` sherds at the dig. In the cities, vanilla's own `ward` and `silence` trims and disc fragments, untouched.
- **What a player might notice:** the dyed coat on the body at the foot of the city stair is the one at the camp above.
- **Not retold:** who built the cities (canon says endermen; nothing in game says so).

### 3. The stone gate and the road

Canon: E11, E12, L1, L2, L6–L8, M14, M15, P3. How people came and moved: the gate, the paths between settlements, the passes, the ford.

- **Anchor: overworld.** Near spawn, the stone gate: a weathered, partial frame of `mossy_stone_bricks` (the overworld's own frame block) with a torch sconce, standing in a village's oldest corner.
- **Satellites:** the_blossom_gardens (its central gate, everything built round it); the_highland_crossing (guide posts on the passes); the_whitestone_ford (lighthouses at the crossing); the_stonemantle (the quarries that supplied the road).
- **Overworld:** the road itself, in fragments: worn paths and road markers (lived-in layer) between villages, over passes and down to fords, more often near spawn and thinning outward.
- **Marks:** the road marker (a lantern on a post over a mossy stone-brick footing), the same in each world; mossy stone bricks as the founding stone in the oldest building of each settlement on the trail; `friend` and `sheaf` sherds.
- **What a player might notice:** the lantern posts on the pass in another world are the ones by the road home.
- **Not retold:** where the humans came from.

### 4. The soldiers who stayed

Canon: E15, P13, X3. A kingdom's watch posts, still manned by its soldiers' heirs, who became what they guarded against.

- **Anchor: the_lost_outpost.** The outpost, the bandit towers, the barracks, the old fort.
- **Satellites:** the_sun_kingdoms (the bandits' village at the edge); the_highland_crossing (a border keep).
- **Overworld:** a few pillager outposts in the frontier band (3072–5632) carry the trail's marks; the rest stay plain vanilla.
- **Marks:** two banners in one place: the ominous banner flown outside, and inside, kept, the old kingdom's banner (a fixed design used only on this trail); iron armour with the `sentry` trim on remains at the old fort and the keep; training targets and cages.
- **What a player might notice:** the old banner inside the bandit tower hangs over the door of the keep by the pass.
- **Not retold:** the kingdom's name, or why it fell.

### 5. The lessons

Canon: E10, P8, P9, M13. A teaching that went too far, and the people it changed.

- **Anchor: the_crystal_vale.** The wizard towers where the lessons happened.
- **Satellites:** the_darkpine_depths (where the listeners withdrew); the_greywoods (the witches' houses); the_roothold (the experiment the forest grew round).
- **Overworld:** swamp huts and the mansion near the border carry the marks.
- **Marks:** an enderman's materials where a villager built: a purpur step or an amethyst block in a hut's hearth; a lectern with a blank book; ender pearls kept in a pot; brewing stands; remains with the `vex` trim (vanilla's mansion trim); `brewer` sherds.
- **What a player might notice:** purpur in a witch's hut, a long way from any End.
- **Not retold:** what was taught.

### 6. The gold that sank

Canon: E5, E6, L4, M4, M5, M6. A piglin empire: its capital, its holy places, and the older city the lava took.

- **Anchor: the_gilded_pit.** The capital: the ledge, the bastion, the forge, the palace, the forbidden castle.
- **Satellites:** the_buried_age (the older city under lava, Pompeii-style digs); the_weeping_vault (the seal); the_obsidian_sanctum (the holy places); the_slatemouth (canon: what it swallows it puts down in the capital).
- **Overworld:** vanilla's ruined portals, with their gold and netherrack, are this trail's echo as they are; nothing added.
- **Marks:** gilded blackstone in the walls of the trail's places only; a crying-obsidian seal over a small sealed door in each satellite (canon M5); the same treasure-room arrangement (gold blocks round a single chest) in the capital and the buried city.
- **What a player might notice:** the sealed crying-obsidian door in the buried city is the one in the vault.
- **Not retold:** what the vault holds (P18).

### 7. What grew

Canon: E1, P10, P11, M10. The oldest places, which nobody built.

- **Anchor: the_aviary.** The coral towers, the grown vault, the birdcages.
- **Satellites:** the_snowbound_isle and the_pale_reach (towers, birdcages, a vault).
- **Overworld:** one empty birdcage on the highest summit in the world, once.
- **Marks:** the birdcage itself (iron bars on a calcite foot, empty); vaults with no door, grown from the rock. No banners, no gear, no words; the absence of a maker is the mark.
- **What a player might notice:** an empty birdcage on a mountain top, and the same on an island in the sky.
- **Not retold:** anything at all.

## Told where they stand

These canon events stay in their own dimension and link to nothing. A world where everything connects is as artificial as one where nothing does.

- **The quiet endings** (E16): the burned grove, the unfinished festival, the swallowed marsh towns, the dried farm, the failed land. Each is its own small mystery.
- **The failed crossings** (E17): a wreck or two in the_shallows, the_frozen_strait, the_tidepools.
- **The sealed and worshipped places** (P18, P20): the_wuthering_wisteria's gate, the_abyssal_shrine.
- **The endermen's void worlds** (E2, E4, X1): the_tiled_expanse, the_pillared_void, the_red_monument, the_starwell. Their one recurring object is the monolith (`mes:monolith`), already placed in each.
- **The bleed and the disintegration** (E18, E19): terrain, not places; the worlds show it in their ground.
- **The greatsword** (M9, X4): `mns:sword` stands in the_twisted_groves, the_fungal_lanterns, the_basalt_spires and the_teal_corruption, unexplained.
- **The_canvas** carries nothing, by its description.

## Portal studies and found recipes

Each portal study (RB-41) belongs to the target dimension's trail when it has one, and stands alone otherwise. It is the one place where words appear: a lectern with a few fragmentary notes in the voice of someone who never understood it either (RB-43), never naming the world beyond. The recipe clues in loot (RB-40) are wordless where they can be: a chest whose slots lay out the frame's shape in its block, with the igniter's precursor in the middle.
